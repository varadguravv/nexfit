PARTLY TRUE

More protein automatically means more muscle.

Protein supports muscle growth, but simply consuming more and more protein does not guarantee additional muscle. Training, total nutrition and recovery matter.

02
MYTH

Lifting weights automatically makes you bulky.

Resistance training can produce many different adaptations depending on training, nutrition, genetics and overall goals.

READ THE VERDICT →
03
MYTH

The more you sweat, the more fat you burn.

Sweat primarily helps regulate body temperature. A sweaty workout is not automatically a better fat-loss workout.

READ THE VERDICT →
04
MYTH

Ab exercises directly burn belly fat.

Training your abdominal muscles strengthens them, but fat loss cannot be precisely directed to one body area simply by exercising that area.

READ THE VERDICT →
05
MYTH

If you're not sore, your workout didn't work.

Muscle soreness can occur after training, especially when a stimulus is new, but soreness is not a required measurement of a productive workout.

READ THE VERDICT →
06
MYTH

Cardio automatically kills your gains.

Cardio and resistance training can coexist. The important variables include training volume, recovery, nutrition and how the sessions are structured.

READ THE VERDICT →
07
MYTH

Eating carbs at night automatically makes you fat.

Body composition is influenced by overall energy intake, activity and nutrition patterns rather than a single clock time at which carbohydrates are eaten.

READ THE VERDICT →
08
MYTH

Sleep doesn't matter if your training is good.

Training is only one part of progress. Adequate sleep supports recovery and helps you maintain consistent performance over time.

01 / CONSISTENCY

Keep showing up.

Sustainable training performed consistently beats constantly changing programs and chasing shortcuts.
