01 / THE FOUNDATION

How Muscle Growth Works

Hypertrophy is the increase in muscle size produced through training stimulus, recovery and adequate nutrition.

I

Type I — Slow Twitch

Highly fatigue-resistant fibers with a high mitochondrial density. They are suited to endurance work, higher repetitions and prolonged muscular tension.

II

Type II — Fast Twitch

High-force fibers that respond strongly to mechanical tension, progressive overload, heavy training and explosive movements.

02 / ANATOMY

Major Muscle Groups

Learn what each major muscle group does, how it is trained, typical recovery guidance and practical movement choices.

01 48–72 H RECOVERY

Chest

PECTORALIS MAJOR & MINOR

Growth: Horizontal adduction and arm flexion. The chest can respond well to controlled pressing and loaded stretch.

Best movements:

Bench Press Incline Press Chest Dips Cable Flyes
02 48–72 H RECOVERY

Back

LATS · RHOMBOIDS · TRAPS

Growth: Combine vertical pulling for lat width with horizontal rowing for mid-back thickness and scapular retraction.

Best movements:

Pull-Ups Barbell Rows Lat Pulldowns Cable Rows
03 48 H RECOVERY

Shoulders

ANTERIOR · LATERAL · REAR DELTS

Growth: Overhead pressing emphasizes the front delts, lateral raises target side-delt width, while reverse-fly patterns train the rear delts.

Best movements:

Overhead Press Lateral Raises Face Pulls Arnold Press
04 48 H RECOVERY

Arms

BICEPS · TRICEPS · FOREARMS

Growth: Biceps are trained through elbow flexion and supination. Triceps respond to pressing and elbow-extension movements.

Best movements:

Barbell Curls Hammer Curls Pushdowns Skull Crushers
05 24–48 H RECOVERY

Abs

RECTUS ABDOMINIS & OBLIQUES

Growth: Trained through spinal flexion and core bracing. Controlled resistance and constant tension can provide an effective stimulus.

Best movements:

Cable Crunches Hanging Leg Raises Russian Twists Ab Wheel
06 48 H RECOVERY

Neck

CERVICAL FLEXORS & EXTENSORS

Growth: Direct resistance and controlled isometric work can train the neck musculature.

Best movements:

Neck Harness Flexion Isometric Holds
07 ~72 H RECOVERY

Legs

QUADS · HAMSTRINGS · GLUTES · CALVES

Growth: Large lower-body movements combine knee flexion/extension and hip-hinge patterns with progressive overload.

Best movements:

Squats RDLs Leg Press Bulgarian Split Squats
03 / TRAIN SMART

Injury Prevention Guide

Common training problems, contributing factors, practical prevention strategies and habits to avoid.

Rotator Cuff / Impingement

Root causes: Overuse of pressing, insufficient upper-back/rear-delt work and poor scapular control.
Helpful habits: Face pulls, band pull-aparts and controlled external-rotation work.
Avoid: Heavy behind-the-neck pressing and uncontrolled lowering of weights.

Lower Back Strain

Root causes: Poor lifting mechanics, inadequate bracing, excessive loading and repeated lumbar flexion.
Helpful habits: Learn the hip hinge, use appropriate loads and develop effective trunk bracing.
Avoid: Ego lifting and intentionally rounding the spine under heavy loads.

Patellar Tendonitis

Root causes: Rapid increases in jumping, squatting or lunge volume and inadequate recovery.
Helpful habits: Progress training gradually and address relevant mobility and strength limitations.
Avoid: Skipping warm-ups or continuing through sharp, acute pain.

Tennis / Golfer's Elbow

Root causes: Repetitive gripping, excessive wrist loading or sudden increases in training volume.
Helpful habits: Gradual loading, appropriate grip selection and progressive forearm strengthening.
Avoid: Ignoring persistent pain or repeatedly training through worsening symptoms.
04 / RECOVER

Nutrition That Supports Training

Training creates the stimulus. Recovery and nutrition help provide the resources needed to adapt.

Build Around The Basics

Prioritize sufficient protein, carbohydrate sources that support training, healthy fats and adequate hydration. The existing Nexfit guide uses a protein range of approximately 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day.

Things To Avoid

Avoid crash diets, chronic dehydration and habits that consistently interfere with recovery or training performance.
