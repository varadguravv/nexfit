01
SELECTED MUSCLE

CHEST

The chest is primarily made up of the pectoralis major and minor. Effective training combines pressing and fly-based movements through a controlled range of motion.

RECOVERY WINDOW
48–72 HOURS

Allow adequate recovery before repeatedly training the same muscle intensely.

PRIMARY FOCUS
PRESS + FLY

Horizontal pressing and controlled adduction are the foundation of chest development.

```
```
TRAINING INTELLIGENCE

BUILD IT. DON'T JUST MOVE IT.

How It Grows

Effective chest development requires horizontal adduction of the arm and arm flexion. The muscle responds well to progressive overload and controlled range of motion.

Best Movements

  • Barbell / Dumbbell Bench Press — primary pressing movement.
  • Incline Press — emphasizes upper chest.
  • Chest Dips — compound pressing movement.
  • Cable Flyes — controlled adduction and tension.

Recommended Exercise Library

THE SCIENCE

MUSCLE GROWTH IS MECHANICAL.

Type I muscle fibers are highly fatigue resistant and respond well to higher repetitions and sustained tension. Type II fibers produce greater force and respond strongly to mechanical tension, progressive overload and explosive work.

TRAIN SMART

INJURY INTELLIGENCE.

01

Rotator Cuff Tendonitis / Impingement

ROOT CAUSE: Overuse of pressing movements, poor scapular stability and insufficient rear-delt / upper-back work.

APPROACH: Include face pulls, band pull-aparts and controlled external rotation work.

AVOID: Heavy behind-the-neck pressing and uncontrolled repetitions.

02

Lower Back Strain

ROOT CAUSE: Poor bracing, spinal rounding under load and attempting loads beyond current technique.

APPROACH: Master the hip hinge with manageable loads and develop core control.

AVOID: Lifting with a rounded spine or sacrificing technique for heavier weight.

03

Patellar Tendonitis

ROOT CAUSE: Rapid training-volume increases, repetitive jumping and poor movement mechanics.

APPROACH: Progress gradually and maintain appropriate recovery between demanding leg sessions.

AVOID: Ignoring sharp pain around the kneecap.

04

Tennis / Golfer's Elbow

ROOT CAUSE: Repetitive gripping and excessive wrist loading.

APPROACH: Gradual forearm conditioning and neutral grips where appropriate.

AVOID: Ignoring persistent elbow discomfort.

RECOVERY STARTS OUTSIDE THE GYM.

Muscle development depends on more than training. Prioritize adequate protein, carbohydrates for training performance, healthy fats and hydration. The original Nexfit guide recommends approximately 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of body weight as a general muscle-building target.
